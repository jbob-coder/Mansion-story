# World Bible

## Setting
`WORLD-001` is **Eirenfall**, a medieval-fantasy reality whose inhabited regions know the Age of High Banners in year 30,000. The name **Bellward** refers to a later frontier march and manor; it **did not** exist at creation. The original empty-repository README used Bellward prematurely; this supersedes that scope without erasing its eventual place in history.

## Tone and design
History accumulates; settlements arise from people, labor, climate, trade, conflict and cooperation. Magic is universal in **potential**, unequal in expression and constrained by material context. Roads can decay, houses can become ruins, and political borders can be contested. No population exists merely to supply player quests.

## World layers
- `LYR-001` Surface: living lands, rivers, fields, settlements.
- `LYR-002` Deep: subsurface stone, mines, ruins and cave networks.
- `LYR-003` Sky: atmosphere, clouds and flying pathways.
- `LYR-004` Veil: distinct metaphysical space, not a higher altitude.
- `LYR-005` Aether: nonlocal source substrate; its existence is part of the cosmological model, but it has **no map coordinates**.

Layers do not imply a rank of moral worth. Traversal between Surface and Deep can be a tunnel; Veil crossings require a specifically registered crossing. Aether cannot be reached by walking.

## Surveyed region at the chosen era
The initial local atlas includes Crownspire (royal seat), Bellward Manor (march seat), Hollowford (village), Silverrun Ford, Ashenwood, Copperfall Mine, Eastwatch Keep, Old Cairn, Starwater Lake and First Ember Sanctum. Names, coordinates and route edges are in the data registry, not in this narrative alone.

## Reality categories
**Confirmed authored fact**, **simulation-observed fact** (none yet), **in-world belief**, **rumor**, **unresolved possibility** and **retconned/superseded fact** are different states. A belief may influence a character regardless of whether it is true.

## Open truths (do not invent answers silently)
The number and extent of other continents, the topology of the world beyond the initial mapped window, the identities of any additional gods, the origin of Veil crossings and all uncharted nations remain unresolved. An NPC's faith must not be used to invent a cosmological certainty.

## Real darkness and the disputed moral world
The Night Archivist (`ENT-001`) is a **real, sapient and deliberately hostile** memory-being in the Veil. It is not proven to be a creator-deity or the origin of all cruelty. Esh Vel's forced oath is the earliest recorded atrocity of its particular kind, not the first evil act in eternity. Distinguish intelligent malice from natural predation; sapient Hornborn are people, not wildlife or a predestined evil race.

The Gloam Chain (`ORG-002`) profits from coercive captivity. The Crownspire Old Houses Council (`ORG-005`) propagates some discriminatory etiquette; the prejudice is attributed to that faction rather than every citizen or the crown itself. The Unbound Watch (`ORG-003`) opposes capture networks without a guaranteed victory. For complete moral and creature canon see [Shadows and Evil](SHADOWS_AND_EVIL.md) and [Beasts, Beastfolk and Taboos](BEASTS_BEASTFOLK_AND_TABOOS.md).

## Human ascendancy at the selected era
Humans (`SPC-005`) are the dominant **sapient civilization across the documented inhabited sphere** through settlement population, farming, protected roads, armies, contracts, and administrative influence. They hold **79.8% of a ten-settlement authored population sample**, which is **not a global measured census**. Other peoples remain autonomous and important. A physically powerful wyvern does not automatically dominate institutions. Explore [Human Dominion](HUMAN_DOMINION.md) and [Creature Codex](CREATURE_CODEX.md). Full societies and production: [Civilization and Economy](CIVILIZATION_AND_ECONOMY.md).
